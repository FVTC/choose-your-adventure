The hardest part of Helen's engagement was that Jed's mother was always taking him off on long trips to warm climes on the plea of his ill health. Yet, dissatisfied with himself and his life, he became a habitual invalid, and Helen's family told her that it was lucky she had not married him.

Helen was forty-six when Jed's mother died, and Jed almost fifty. His invalid habits were firmly set, and although Helen became his wife she often thought that she might just as well be his mother. The couple continued to follow the schedule of travel which the dominating old lady had worked out, because Jed was accustomed to it.

But though Jed was an invalid he kept something of the whimsicality that had always charmed Helen. The consequence was that Helen had a delightful old, age. All that she had lost was twenty-five years of life.
