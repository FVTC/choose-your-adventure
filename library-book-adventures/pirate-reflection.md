🌑 The Mirror Below
==
End Scene
--

You lean over the railing. The mirrored sea below shows not your face, but another’s—older, hollow-eyed, the same coat on their shoulders… the same scar on their cheek.

They look up at you.

“I _told_ you not to come back.”

You stagger backward, but the reflection moves closer. The water doesn’t ripple—it rises. Hands made of liquid glass reach for the ship, for you. The deck tilts, ropes scream, the crew shouts—but their voices distort, drawn out like echoes swallowed by distance.

The book in the barrel slams shut with a final thud. The sound shatters the stillness.

Then the sea pulls.

The ship jerks downward, creaking in protest as the mirrored surface turns black and deep. You grab the helm, but your hands sink through it like mist. The reflection smiles—your smile—and whispers:

“You looked too long.”

A flash of light blinds you. For an instant, you see everything—the storm, the island, the faces of every captain who stood where you are now. Then only darkness.

When the sea calms, there’s no wreckage. No crew. No captain.
Just the faint sound of a voice whispering through the waves:

“Set the course…”

And somewhere in a distant library, a book closes itself—its cover still warm.

_— The End —_
