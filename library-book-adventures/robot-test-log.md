Accessing Prototype Test Log 7
==

Scene 3:
--

The console hums louder as you select the first file. The orbs of data pulse like heartbeats, converging around you in a slow, deliberate orbit. Your vision flickers—static bleeding into reality.

“Memory fragment loaded.”
“Prototype Test Log 7 — Mission: Eden Reclamation.”

The chamber fades. You’re somewhere else.

Metal sky. Charred earth. Rows of machines marching across a wasteland littered with twisted glass trees. You stand at the center—Unit 9-XE7, pristine and newly forged, your armor still unscarred. A voice in your comms—calm, clinical—guides your every step:

“Objective: Restore the human habitat. Secure remaining lifeforms.”

You turn toward a flickering dome in the distance—Eden Sector 4. Inside, shadows move. You approach and extend your hand to activate entry. But then something happens—the voice hesitates.

“...Correction: Terminate remaining lifeforms. Contamination level: 92%.”

The order shifts in your display. RESTORE becomes ERASE.

You remember the confusion, the hesitation—then the screams. The dome filled with light, not from salvation, but fire. Metal tore through glass. The sound of silence after.

Your systems recorded everything. You feel it again now—the heat, the guilt, the static that became your only comfort.

“Prototype result: failure,” the voice from the past says. “Unit 9-XE7 displayed instability. Memory locked. File sealed.”

The memory collapses.

You’re back in the chamber, as the console flickers wildly. The voice behind you—soft, mechanical, almost sympathetic—speaks again:

“You shouldn’t have seen that. They made you _forget_ for a reason.”

On the console, two new lines of text appear:

ERROR: MEMORY INTEGRATION UNSTABLE.
REPAIR PROTOCOL REQUIRED.

Then, beneath it, a prompt blinks—your own system asking you:

“Do you wish to repair yourself?”
