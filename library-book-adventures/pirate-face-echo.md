🌀The Echo Ashore
==
End Scene:
--

You give the command.

“Drop anchor.”

The crew hesitates—but the ship does not. The chains unspool like memory itself, vanishing into water as smooth as obsidian. The ship halts just beyond the golden mist.

You descend alone.

The longboat touches the glowing shore with barely a sound. The sand is warm, humming beneath your boots. The mist parts around you, and you see no trees, no ruins—only shapes that flicker and vanish the moment you focus.

Then a mirror rises from the sand.

It is not made of glass, but water held upright, trembling. And in it: yourself.

Not as you are—but as you were.

Younger. Smaller. Barefoot and storm-eyed. The person you were before the sea called your name. They speak first.

“You left us.”

“I became something else,” you say.

They shake their head. “You became everyone’s memory. But you never faced your own.”

The wind howls suddenly. The mist becomes voices, faces—those you’ve lost, those you’ve left. _And the book._ Every choice you buried. Every truth you twisted to survive.

The echo calls again:

“Why did you return?”

You step toward the mirror.

“I returned to remember,” you say. “And to choose.”

You reach into the water. It resists, then accepts.

In that moment, you choose to stay and the island explodes in golden flame—not fire, but release. Light races across the sea like a sunrise. Back on the ship, the crew gasps as the sky clears and the waters ripple with new wind.

You return, but not as the captain they thought you were.

You return whole, reclaiming your identity.

The echo is silent at last.

_— The End —_
