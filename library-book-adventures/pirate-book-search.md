Scene 2: 📖 Try to find the book again and wake up?
==

_I'm not supposed to be here , what do I do!?_

You spot a weather-worn chest bolted to the floor near the ladder. The brass lock is cracked, and something inside glints when the ship tilts. Maybe what’s inside can help you.



You drop to your knees beside the chest, heart pounding so hard it shakes the lantern light. The ship groans again, timbers creaking like an old throat clearing to speak. Your hands—your _borrowed_ hands—fumble with the broken lock until it gives with a sharp snap.

Inside, the air smells of ink and ozone. There’s no gold, no maps this time—only a single book, its cover **the same glowing red as the one that brought you here**. It hums faintly, the light pulsing in time with your heartbeat.

*“I’m not supposed to be here…”* you whisper. *“What do I do!?”*

As if it hears you, the pages flutter open on their own. The words rearrange, ink sliding like liquid:

**“TO LEAVE, YOU MUST REMEMBER.”**

Images flash—your hands on the library shelf, the book’s weight in your palm, the scent of dust and paper. You can *feel* the library around you again, faint but close. The ship tilts violently, stormwinds howling through the cracks.

You grip the glowing page and shout, “I remember!”

Light erupts from the chest, red spilling into white, and for an instant you see everything at once—the ship, the storm, the faces of the crew frozen mid-motion—before they dissolve like ink in water.

Then—

_Silence._

You’re back in the library, on your knees, the book closed before you. Its glow is gone. Only faint scorch marks remain on the cover, and when you touch them, they’re warm.

A single line of text has appeared on the open page:

_*Every story knows its reader.*_

You stare, breath unsteady, heart still half at sea. The air smells faintly of salt.

And somewhere deep inside, a voice you can’t quite forget whispers:

 “Until next time… Captain.”

 _Yeah, I dont think so._ You think to yourself as you quickly replace the book.
