# You decide that this attack is uncharacteristic

Karl has always been, at worst, a minor nuiscance, not a real threat. Why would he even attack a village. That's way riskier than anything Karl has ***EVER*** done before. This detail bothers you too much. You decide you need to get to bottom of this.
> Karl! Stop this attack at once!

Karl's army stops advancing and aims their bows at you. Karl responds:
> Out of my way! I have a village to conquer!

You stand your ground.
> This isn't like you, man! Why are you doing this?

One of the goblins fires an arrow at you. You can't tell if that was intentional or if that particular goblin just has bad aim. Karl speaks:
> Last warning, dragonborn! Get out of my way! I need this village to protect my people from the dragon!

Did he just say dragon? Is he talking about the dragon in the mountains you were about to go slay?
> If you are refering to the dragon up in the mountains, I was just on my way up there to deal with it. You don't need this village to protect your people. You just need to trust me to do my job.

Karl's army stops marching. Eventually, he gives the order to stop the invasion.
> Troops, turn around! Let's return home and let the adventurer handle that dragon. But know this, adventurer! If I see that dragon again, I will return to this village and destroy everything!

Great. This is just what you wanted, more pressure to succeed. But at least the village is safe.
