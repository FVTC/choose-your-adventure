# General Plans

This document should be used to write down the overarching ideas and themes for each main route. It can be a vague or detailed as you like.

If you want to make any additions or edits to routes that aren't your main one, **please reference this document first** to make sure your additions are in line with the overall plan.

Things like the location, what the dragon looks like, and details about the POV character can be different in each path. All details are influenced by the first choice the reader makes.

## Slay Route
### Micah
*Micah, feel free to change what you want with this, these are just what my first thoughts on this route were.*

**Genre:** Eldritch Horror

**Theme:** Inevitability

- All branching routes eventually converge on one single ending.
- The horror aspect is more important than it specifically being eldritch, but I love playing with eldritch horror. 
    - The core of eldritch horror is becoming/being shown something unknowable and beyond human comprehension, and being forced to know it anyways. This often leads to madness, Yay!
    - Threats are mainly mental stability or to your understanding of the world, the horror comes from psychological terror rather than gore
- From this point on, every time you try to make a logical or reasonable choice it is the Wrong One.
- You already chose to react to something you didn't understand with violence, and you cannot undo it. 
- Every time there seems to be some logic or pattern, it is immediately turned on its head. Except after this has repeated a few times, a pattern can stay for just long enough to subvert the player's expectation of expectations always being subverted :)

## Freeze Route
### Ray

**Genre:** Survival

**Theme:** Regret, inaction

- Inaction is not neutral, it is still as much of a choice as action and still has consequences
- Pseudo time loop (either the main route or a sub-route)
    - You get to what appears to be the first page again, with the same options, but this time whatever you choose doesn't have a meaningful difference to the outcome.
    - You find yourself unable to move, or the dragon still dies, or the dragon frees itself before you can do anything.
    - You chose to remove yourself from this story, and so this story will continue to unfold without you.
- You find youself facing different threats to your physical well-being
- Choices can be categorized as "passive" or "active", if you remain passive you end up in the pseudo-time-loop. If you make active choices, something else, I haven't thought that far
